    How prices and scores work
    • Price = listed price + postage + 10% GST for overseas sellers (shown as "GST incl." when the seller's shipping already includes it; over A$1,000 it's paid at the border). Postage not shown on the listing is estimated: A$12 in Australia.
    • Sold benchmark = recent sold prices for the same card, language and grade (PriceCharting, converted at the current exchange rate), cross-checked against Japanese and Australian sales.
    • Deal score (0–100): value under market (40), how fully the listing was confirmed (20), grade (15: PSA 10 = 15, 9 = 12, Near Mint raw = 9), benchmark quality (10), seller and origin (15: Australia highest, then Asia).
    • The bar on each listing runs from A$0 on one scale: the fill is what you'd pay, the solid line is the recent sold price (two lines mark a sold range), and the dashed line is the bid or offer limit.
    • Bid up to / Offer around keeps the card 15% under market after postage and GST.
    • Anniversary reprints: cards reprinted with the original art are checked on the item page and tagged "Original, not reprint ✓". How to tell them apart: Celebrations 25th (2021): same number, small 25th logo beside the art; PSA label says "Celebrations Classic Collection", originals say "1999 Pokemon Game". 30th Celebration (2026): gold border, "30" Pikachu stamp. Evolutions (2016): numbered x/108. Japanese CP6 20th Anniversary (2016): x/087 with a 1st Edition mark and modern back. Japanese 25th Anniversary Collection (2021): x/028 and promos x/025. Originals have the old back and no set total. Generated eBay searches already exclude these words.
    • Budget: graded cards landing over A$1,000 are hidden by default unless a want line sets a higher budget. Use the "Graded over A$1,000 hidden" button to see them.
    • Rules: pre-2018 cards, except anything on your want list (any year); no US, UK or Canadian sellers; non-holo cards only when graded 10; every listing re-opened on its own page {VERIFIED}; overpriced, low-grade and conflicting listings are removed rather than shown.

        Exclusives worth hunting limited runs that never hit normal shelves

          Is it really sealed? check before you pay

          • Weighed packs: heavier WOTC and early modern packs are more likely to hold a holo, and some sellers weigh packs and sell on the light ones. "Unweighed" is only a claim; buy loose vintage packs from sellers who sell whole boxes or have long feedback, or buy packs graded sealed by PSA or CGC.
          • Resealed packs: look for uneven or doubled crimps, glue shine, wrinkles near the seams and a pack that looks puffed or flat. Compare the crimp to photos of a known-genuine pack of the same set.
          • Boxes and ETBs: factory shrink is tight with neat, consistent folds. Loose, cloudy or messy-seamed wrap, or wrap that doesn't match known-factory photos of that product, is a reseal warning. Japanese factory shrink has small air holes.
          • Price too good: a sealed box at half its sold value is almost always resealed, weighed, fake or not real stock. Treat it like a too-good slab.
          • Anniversary and modern reprints: Celebrations, 25th and 30th anniversary products and Evolutions copy vintage art. Check the set name and year on the wrapper, not just the art.
          • Pay safely: eBay or PayPal Goods & Services only for private sales; ask for a video of the product turning in the light before paying for anything over a few hundred dollars.

          • Graded: tap Verify cert (or look up the cert at psacard.com) and match the card and grade to the photo. Several listings here had a title grade that didn't match the item details.

          Limits

          • Instagram-only and Facebook-only dealers are mostly invisible to search, so small vintage sellers are underrepresented.
          • Shops on BinderPOS-style software list every English set in their menus. A Base Set or EX menu entry alone didn't earn more than a 3.
          • Stock moves fast. Scores reflect what a seller tends to carry, not today's exact stock.
          • No vintage-focused shop was found on the NSW Mid North Coast; the nearest walk-in options are Newcastle and Brisbane.
